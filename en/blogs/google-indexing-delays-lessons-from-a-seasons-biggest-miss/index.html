Why indexing is the first SEO step you must not ignore

Author: Adrians StankevičsPublished: 8 August 2026Updated: 23 October 20265 min read

Indexing means that Google has saved your page in its database and can show it in the search results. If a page is not indexed, nobody will find it, however good its content is. That is why I check indexing before everything else — before keywords, content and links.

When your most valuable content is invisible to Google

The first time I ran into an indexing delay was exactly when a client's site was at its peak demand of the season. The content that was supposed to attract visitors did not appear in search at all. The cause was duplicate pages.

Google saw several versions of the same page and did not understand which one to index. As a result, none of them was indexed. Since then, indexing has been the first thing I check on every project.

Good content, well-chosen keywords and strong backlinks achieve nothing if Google does not know your page exists.

What indexing means in practice

Google works in three stages. First the bot finds and reads the page, then it saves it in the index, and only after that can the page rank in the search results. If the second stage fails, the third does not happen.

If you write content to attract organic visitors but the page is not indexed, that work brings no results. This applies to blog articles as well as service pages.

A page that is already indexed can also drop out of the index. The most common causes are duplicate pages, incorrect canonical tags and technical errors. A canonical tag is a note in the page code that says which of several similar pages is the original version.

What to check so pages do not drop out of the index

From my experience, I recommend regularly checking whether your most important pages are still indexed. Pay particular attention to these things:

  • whether there are duplicate versions of a page, for example with and without “www”, or with “http” and “https”;
  • whether each page has a correct canonical tag, so that Google understands which version is the original;
  • whether you have requested indexing in Google Search Console after publishing a new page.

These are basic technical SEO checks. They do not take much time, but they prevent a problem that can make all your other work worthless.

How to check whether a page is indexed

The quickest way is to type “site:” and your domain into Google Search, for example “site:yoursite.lv”. Google will show the pages from that website that are in the index. It is a rough check, but it quickly shows if any important pages are missing.

Google Search Console gives a more precise answer. There you can enter a specific URL and see whether the page is in the index, when Google last visited it and why it may not be indexed. You can also request re-indexing there.

Search Console also shows an overall report for all pages on the website. It shows how many pages are indexed and which ones Google has skipped, for example as duplicates or pages with an error.

How to speed up indexing

When I publish a new article on my blog, Google usually indexes it in less than an hour. That is not only thanks to the right technical settings. The website's age and the trust Google gives it also play a role.

If your website is new, expect indexing to take longer. Still, you can speed up the process:

  • submit a sitemap.xml file in Google Search Console;
  • request indexing for every important URL;
  • publish content regularly, so that the Google bot visits your website more often.

The sitemap also needs to be checked later, not just once. When auditing the LatSEO website itself, I discovered that the sitemap file contained only 10 of 40 pages. Google had to find the rest on its own.

How I monitor indexing

I open Google Search Console every morning. There I can see which pages are indexed, which are not and why. If an important page drops out of the index, I notice it quickly and can react before the loss becomes large.

Search Console is a free tool, and it can be connected to any website. If this has not been done for your website yet, start there.

AI Overview makes indexing even more important

Since Google introduced AI Overview, users get some answers directly in the search results. As a result, people click on pages less, because they can read the information they need without opening the website.

That does not mean indexing loses its importance. Quite the opposite: only content that Google has indexed can appear in its summary. After AI Overview was introduced, I started optimising pages for this kind of visibility too, which is called GEO.

So the task is twofold. The page has to be indexed, and its content has to be useful enough that the reader wants to open the website rather than settle for the short answer.

What to do next

If you want your pages to be indexed quickly and reliably, start with the technical basics. Make sure there are no duplicate pages, that the canonical tags are correct and that the sitemap is submitted and complete. Only after that does it make sense to work on content and keywords.
