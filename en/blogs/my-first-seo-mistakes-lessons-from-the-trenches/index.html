My First SEO Mistakes: Lessons from the Trenches

Author: Adrians Stankevics•~3 min read

Learning SEO the Hard Way: My First Mistakes

In the world of SEO, mistakes are inevitable. But the key is to learn from them and grow. At LatSEO, I've had my fair share of blunders, and I'm here to share some of my earliest lessons with you. From URL redirects gone wrong to the power of EEAT, here's what I've learned.

The URL Redirect Disaster That Cost Me Money

One of the first SEO mistakes I remember vividly is a URL redirect error. I had set up Google Ads, but they were pointing to an old URL that resulted in a 404 error. This meant that anyone clicking on my ads was landing on a dead page. It took me four days to realize what was happening, and in that time, I lost a significant amount of ad spend. It was a costly lesson, but it taught me the importance of double-checking all URLs and redirects before launching any campaign.

Using Google Keyword Planner to Understand Search Intent

To understand which keywords people were searching for and how to compete, I turned to Google Keyword Planner. This tool became my go-to for keyword research. It helped me identify high-volume keywords and gauge the competition. It's a free and powerful tool that I still use today to inform my SEO strategies.

Waiting for Organic Traffic: A Test of Patience

When I built my own website, I initially made it purely informational, not expecting people to find it. As a result, it took about a month and a half to see my first organic traffic. Once I revamped the content and applied proper SEO techniques, the flow started. A better example is our client lazergriezeji.lv - people started finding that site organically in less than a month, and they were already calling to inquire about products. This shows that with the right SEO, you can see results much faster.

My First Client: From Employee to SEO Expert

My first client was actually the company I used to work for. I was building their websites, and in the process, I naturally learned about SEO. It was a great starting point because I already understood their business and could experiment with different SEO tactics in a real-world environment.

The SEO Foundation I Initially Ignored: EEAT

In the beginning, I overlooked the importance of EEAT (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness). Now, I consider it the most critical factor for ranking. Google wants to show content that is not only relevant but also credible and trustworthy. Building EEAT involves creating high-quality content, earning backlinks from reputable sources, and showcasing your expertise. It's not something you can fake, and it takes time to build, but it's essential for long-term SEO success.

Key Takeaways for Your SEO Journey

  • Always double-check your URLs and redirects to avoid costly 404 errors.
  • Use Google Keyword Planner to understand search volume and competition.
  • Be patient with organic traffic - it can take time, but with the right SEO, results will come.
  • Learn from your work experience - it can be a valuable training ground.
  • Don't ignore EEAT - it's crucial for ranking and building trust with your audience.
