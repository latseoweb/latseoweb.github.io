Why SEO is not just about keywords: the lesson from my first project

Author: Adrians StankevičsPublished: 3 August 2026Updated: 23 October 20264 min read

SEO is not just about keywords, because Google first has to be able to find and index your page, and only then does it evaluate the content. Keywords help, but without a technically sound page, quality content and visibility in AI tools, their impact is small. I realised this on my very first project.

It all started with one mistake

When I took on my first project — bratus.lv — I thought SEO was all about keywords. I focused on getting the right phrases into the right places in the text.

It helped a little, but only a little. Only later, when I learned about E-E-A-T and GEO, did I realise how wrong I had been. I changed my approach, and the site started performing better.

The danger of keywords: when more means worse

I have seen many pages so packed with keywords that the text sounds unnatural. People notice it immediately, and it hurts both the user experience and trust in the brand.

From a technical point of view, keywords like these do have some effect. But it is like treating the symptom rather than the cause. My conclusion: keywords are one of the tools, not the goal itself.

A good example is the audit of my own website, latseo.com, in October 2026. For the query “seo pakalpojumi” (SEO services), the page received 1,674 impressions and 0 clicks in 90 days. The keyword was on the page, Google showed the page, but people did not click on it. A phrase alone is not enough.

That is why I now open Google Search Console every morning and look not only at rankings but also at impressions and clicks. If a page is shown but nobody clicks on it, the problem is usually in the title, the description or whether the page matches the searcher's intent.

E-E-A-T: why it matters who writes

E-E-A-T stands for Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness and Trustworthiness. Google uses these principles to evaluate content quality.

In practice, this means the text must answer the reader's question, and it must be clear who wrote it and why they can be trusted. That is why I write about what I have done myself instead of retelling general advice. Read more about how I create content on the content strategy page.

GEO: visibility in artificial intelligence tools

Today, visibility on Google alone is no longer enough. More and more people ask their questions to ChatGPT and other large language models (LLMs). If your page cannot be found there, some potential customers simply do not see you.

GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) means structuring content so that AI tools can understand and cite it. When Google introduced AI Overview — an AI summary at the top of the search results — I started optimising for GEO as well. I check how AI tools interpret my content and adjust it so that it is clear, structured and gives direct answers.

Technical SEO: the foundation without which nothing works

Technical SEO is the groundwork on which everything else rests. If a page is not technically sound, the Google bot cannot move through it efficiently. Then the page is not indexed correctly, and even the best keywords do not help. I explain in more detail how Google finds and indexes pages in the article on how Google Search works.

The same audit showed this well. The latseo.com sitemap contained only 10 of 40 pages, and 6 internal links led to non-existent (404) pages. Keywords do not fix errors like these.

The technical foundation I check on every project:

  • clean code and fast loading (Core Web Vitals);
  • a user-friendly mobile version;
  • a clear URL structure;
  • a correct heading hierarchy;
  • an SSL certificate.

A structure like this is a road map for both Google bots and users. Read what exactly this work includes on the technical SEO page.

How I show clients that SEO is not just keywords

When I talk to clients about this myth, I do not just tell them — I show them specific measurements. In Google Search Console I show the average position for the whole site, not just for individual keywords.

That way it becomes visible that the site works as a whole, not as separate details. A site can improve overall even if the rankings of individual keywords do not change. What matters is overall performance, not a single phrase.

How these lessons changed my approach

I no longer start with keywords. I start with an analysis that includes a technical audit, a content quality assessment and GEO opportunities. Only once the basics are in order do I turn to keywords, and I choose them with Google Keyword Planner.

Content and technical optimisation are closely linked. Good content does not work without a technical foundation, and a technically sound page does not work without content that answers users' questions.

Conclusion

If you still think SEO is just about keywords, it is worth taking a wider view. Start with the technical foundation, pay attention to content quality and do not forget about visibility in AI tools.
