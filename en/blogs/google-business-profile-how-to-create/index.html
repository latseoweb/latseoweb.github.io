How to create your business's “digital business card” on Google: Google Business Profile step by step

Author: Adrians StankevičsPublished: 27 July 2026Updated: 23 October 20265 min read

Google Business Profile is a free business profile that shows your name, address, opening hours and phone number in Google Search and Maps. To create one, open google.com/business, find or create your business, verify ownership and fill in your contact details, opening hours and photos.

Before talking about marketing strategies, social media algorithms and paid ads, you need to get the basics in order. Namely, what happens when someone types your business name or the service you provide into Google.

If your business does not appear with a map, opening hours and a phone number, the customer will most likely choose a competitor who is visible. This information panel is called a Google Business Profile (formerly Google My Business). It is free, and it is the foundation of local SEO. I wrote more about what local SEO is in the article “Local SEO: what is it and does your business need it?”.

Below are the steps you can use to set up your profile today.

Step 1: find or create your profile

Open google.com/business and click “Manage now”. Type in your business name and check whether Google already finds it.

Google sometimes creates a business profile automatically from public data. If your business is already listed, you need to claim the profile, that is, prove that you are the owner. If your business is not there, create a profile from scratch.

Google will then ask you to verify that the business really belongs to you. Google itself offers the verification method, and until the profile is verified, it may not be fully visible in search. That is why it is best to complete this step straight away.

Step 2: fill in the “big three” (NAP)

In SEO this is called NAP — Name, Address, Phone number. These details must be accurate and identical everywhere your business is mentioned.

  • Name. Enter the business name exactly as customers know it. Do not add “SIA” or “AS” unless it is part of the brand.
  • Address. Give the exact address so that Google Maps shows the right door.
  • Phone and website. Add your current phone number and a link to your website.

Check that the name, address and phone number in the profile match what is written on your website. If the details differ, it is harder for both the customer and Google to understand which information is correct. If you do not have a website yet to send profile visitors to, you can work out the price in the website development calculator.

If you travel to customers

If you do not receive customers at your address but travel to them instead, for example as a plumber or an electrician, you can keep the address private. Instead, specify a service area — the cities or regions where you work.

Step 3: choose the right category

In the profile you need to set the main category of your business, for example “Hair salon” or “Accountant”. The category helps Google understand which searches your business matches. Choose the one that most precisely describes your main activity, not the broadest one possible.

In the description, write briefly and clearly what you do and where you work. Write for a person, not for a search engine, and do not stuff the text with keywords.

Step 4: add accurate opening hours

Customers get very annoyed when they arrive at a store that turns out to be closed, even though Google says “Open”. So check that the opening hours in your profile match the real ones.

Add holiday opening hours as well. If your opening hours change, update them in the profile on the same day.

Step 5: add 3–5 good-quality photos

People also choose based on what they see. Add a photo of the building's front so the place is easier to find, photos of the interior and a few of your products or your work process.

Use your own, real photos, not stock images. That way the customer sees in advance where they will arrive and what they will get.

Step 6: ask for reviews and reply to them

Once the profile is ready, ask satisfied customers to leave a review. Reply to reviews, including critical ones, calmly and to the point. That way the next customer sees that the business is active and responsive.

Your task for today

Do not put it off until tomorrow. Open your Google profile and check that all the information is accurate: name, address, phone, website, opening hours and photos. It is a small but important step towards new customers.

Is Google Business Profile free?

Yes. Creating and maintaining a profile on Google is free.

Can I create a profile without a public address?

Yes. If you travel to customers and do not receive them at your address, you can hide the address and specify a service area.

Does a Google Business Profile replace a website?

No. The profile helps people find you in Google Search and Maps, but on your website you can explain your services and prices in more detail. Both work best together, as long as the details in them match.
