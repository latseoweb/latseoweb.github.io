Video audit: why I look at the title and description first, not the tools

Author: Adrians StankevičsPublished: 6 August 2026Updated: 23 October 20265 min read

In every video SEO audit, I first check the page title and meta description, because they are SEO basics and are most often not optimised. After that I look at the technical elements and backlinks. In this article I explain what exactly I check, why tools do not show everything and what you can check yourself.

What a video audit is and how I make it

I record video audits myself and send them to the client. I open your website, go through it, explain what I see and recommend what to improve. The audit is free, and I send it within 24 hours.

I chose the video format because it lets me show a problem on screen and explain right away why it matters. You get my recommendations, not just an automatic tool report.

A video audit is not a full technical audit. Its purpose is to show where the first and most obvious problems are, so that you can decide where to start.

The first thing I notice: the title and meta description

The first thing I usually notice is an unoptimised meta title and description. The title is the page name that Google shows as the link in the search results. The meta description is the short description below it.

If these elements are not in order, there are most likely problems elsewhere too. So I start with them and say how they could be improved.

An example: if a fishing tackle store's title is “Labākās pīckas Latvijā” (using a colloquial word for fishing rods), it should be changed to “Labākās makšķeres Latvijā” (the standard word people actually search for) or a similar phrase. People search for the words they use themselves, and the title has to match them. A fix like this can be made right away.

Google sometimes shows a different text snippet from the page instead of the meta description. Still, a well-written description with a clear offer gives people a reason to choose your link rather than the competitor's link next to it.

Why tools do not show keyword relevance

Tools such as Ahrefs or Google Keyword Planner show a keyword's search volume and competition. They cannot directly assess whether a particular keyword really fits your page and your customers. This judgement is partly subjective.

That is why I assess keyword relevance myself, based on my experience. Tools provide the data, but I provide the context and the recommendations on what to do with that data. For example, a title can be technically correct but use a keyword your customers do not search for. A tool will not flag that as an error, but I will notice it.

How I use the Ahrefs toolbar

In the video audit I use the Ahrefs toolbar — a browser extension that quickly shows a page's technical SEO basics. With it I immediately see the title, description, H1 heading, canonical and other elements.

The canonical tells Google which version of a page is the main one when similar content is available at several addresses. The H1 is the page's main heading, and a page should usually have only one.

The Ahrefs toolbar shows the technical picture well, but it does not replace content and keyword analysis. If the website has many pages, I also use Screaming Frog, which checks all pages in one go. I wrote about this work in more detail on the technical SEO page.

Backlinks: the topic I explain most often

Backlinks are links to your website from other websites. Clients ask about them most often, and I have to explain them in more detail.

It is often assumed that more links automatically mean better SEO. Quality and relevance matter more. Backlinks can be compared to votes from other websites, but not all votes are equally valuable. A link from an authoritative industry portal is worth more than many links from low-quality directories.

That is why, in the video audit, I show which backlinks are valuable and which ones should be avoided. I described how I build links for clients on the link building page.

An example: what I found on my own website

I apply the same principles to the LatSEO website. In the October 2026 audit I found that the sitemap file contained only 10 of 40 pages. Six internal links led to 404 pages.

For the query “seo pakalpojumi” (SEO services), the page received 1,674 impressions and 0 clicks in 90 days. This means the page appears in search, but people do not click on it. In a case like this, I check the page's ranking and how its title and description look in the results.

What you can check yourself

  1. Check the title and meta description of every important page. They should be different from each other, include a keyword your customers search for and match the page content.
  2. Make sure each page has one H1 heading.
  3. Use the Ahrefs toolbar or a similar tool to look at the technical elements, including the canonical.
  4. In Google Search Console, check which queries have many impressions but few clicks.
  5. Review which websites link to you, and judge them by quality, not quantity.
