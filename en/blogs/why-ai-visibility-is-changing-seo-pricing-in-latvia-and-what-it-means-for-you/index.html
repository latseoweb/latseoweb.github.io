SEO is not just keywords

A potential client once asked me why SEO costs so much if all you have to do is add a couple of keywords. I hear this idea often. Keywords are only the foundation on which the rest of the work is built.

SEO includes technical optimisation, content planning, competitor research and now also visibility in AI search. When this work is seen as just inserting keywords, it creates pressure on the price that does not match the real scope of work. I wrote more about this in the article about the myth that SEO is just keywords.

Factor 1: the size of your website

The more pages, the more there is to check and fix. An audit of a one-page website with a few sections takes less time than one of an online store with many product and category pages.

Larger websites also have technical problems more often. When auditing the LatSEO website itself, I found that the sitemap file contained only 10 of 40 pages and 6 internal links led to a 404 page. Every error like this has to be found and fixed, and that affects the price.

Factor 2: competition in your industry

In my experience, competition is what makes an SEO project more expensive than anything else. If several businesses in your industry are already working on SEO, you have to research carefully what they are doing and understand how your page can become more useful than theirs.

My own example shows competition well. For the query “seo pakalpojumi” (SEO services), the LatSEO page received 1,674 impressions in 90 days, but not a single click. Being visible is not enough to get a visitor if competitors rank higher.

Factor 3: the scope of work

The price depends on what exactly needs to be done. For one client, a list of technical fixes after the audit is enough; another needs regular work every month: technical SEO, content, local SEO and links.

Professional tools also make up part of the costs. I work with Google Search Console, Google Keyword Planner, Screaming Frog and Ahrefs. I use them to analyse the website, track rankings and look for new opportunities.

An audit or monthly work: where to start

If you do not know what condition your website is in, start with an audit. It shows what prevents Google from finding and understanding the site, and from that it becomes clear how much work lies ahead. After the audit, you can make the fixes yourself, leave them to your developer or to me.

Monthly SEO is suitable if you want the work to continue regularly. That means monitoring the website in Google Search Console, fixing new errors, adding content and tracking rankings. SEO results build up gradually, so one-off work is rarely enough in a competitive industry.

If I develop the website, part of the SEO work is done right from the start. Technically clean code, fast loading, a mobile version, a clear URL structure, a correct heading hierarchy and SSL are included in every project.

How AI search is changing SEO work and pricing

Artificial intelligence has changed how people search for information. Some users look for answers in AI chat tools such as ChatGPT or Gemini, and Google itself shows AI Overview summaries. That is why a website now has to be prepared both for regular Google Search and for AI systems.

After AI Overview was introduced, I started optimising pages for this kind of visibility too, which is called GEO. The amount of work has grown, because the website has to be prepared for two environments. I do not charge extra for AI optimisation; it is already included in the price.

If you do not want to be visible in AI systems, this part of the work can be skipped. However, I do not recommend it, because people who look for answers in AI tools are just as much potential customers as those who search on Google.
