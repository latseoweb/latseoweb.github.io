SEO Basics: My Journey from Zero to First Clients

Author: Adrians Stankevics • ~4 min read

How I Learned SEO the Hard Way

When I started my first SEO project, I thought it was about stuffing keywords and buying links. I was wrong. Within three months, I saw a 40% drop in organic traffic for a client in Riga who sold handmade wooden furniture. That failure taught me more than any course ever did.

Here's the thing: SEO is not a one-time task. It is a continuous process that requires patience, testing, and a solid foundation. In this post, I'll share the lessons I learned from zero to landing my first five clients.

Lesson 1: Technical SEO Is Non-Negotiable

Before you write a single blog post, your website must be technically sound. Google's crawlers need to access and index your pages. If your site has broken links, slow loading times, or poor mobile responsiveness, all your content efforts will be wasted.

For example, a local bakery in Liepaja had a beautiful website, but it took 6 seconds to load on mobile. Over 70% of their mobile visitors left before the page finished loading. After we compressed images and enabled caching, load time dropped to 2 seconds, and their bounce rate fell by 35%.

Technical SEO includes site speed, mobile-friendliness, XML sitemaps, and proper URL structures. If you are not sure where to start, consider a technical SEO audit to identify critical issues.

Lesson 2: Content Is About Intent, Not Keywords

In the beginning, I wrote articles targeting high-volume keywords like "best furniture" and "buy wooden table." Guess what? They ranked nowhere. Then I shifted to long-tail keywords that matched what people actually search for, like "how to choose a solid oak dining table for a small apartment."

This change brought 150% more organic traffic in two months. Why? Because the content answered a specific question with a clear solution. Users found value, stayed longer, and converted.

For content that works, you need a content strategy that maps user intent to each stage of the buyer's journey. Think about what your customer asks before they buy, and answer it thoroughly.

Lesson 3: Patience Pays Off (But Not Forever)

SEO is slow. My first client, a small law firm in Jelgava, saw no results for six weeks. We kept publishing quality content and fixing technical issues. By month four, their organic sessions increased by 200%, and they started receiving leads from Google every week.

But patience has limits. If you have not seen any movement after six months, your strategy is likely flawed. Revisit your keyword choices, analyze competitors, and adjust. You can also rebuild your website if it is holding you back.

Lesson 4: Links Still Matter

Backlinks are not dead. In fact, they remain one of the top three ranking factors. However, quality beats quantity. One link from a reputable local news site gave my furniture client more authority than 20 spammy directory links.

We focused on earning links through guest posts, local partnerships, and PR. For example, we sponsored a local design event, which got us a link from the event website and a mention in a regional blog. That single link boosted our domain rating by 12 points.

If you need help, our link building service can help you acquire high-quality links that move the needle.

Lesson 5: Track Everything, Trust Data

I used to guess what was working. Then I installed Google Search Console and Analytics. The data changed everything. I saw which pages got impressions but no clicks, and which keywords brought actual conversions.

For instance, one client's "price list" page received 500 clicks a month but had a 90% bounce rate. We redesigned it with clear pricing and a call-to-action, and conversions tripled within a month. Data tells you what to fix.

Make data a habit. Check your analytics weekly, not monthly. Set up goals and track them. If you need help interpreting the numbers, our SEO packages include regular reporting and insights.

Final Thoughts: Start With the Basics

SEO is not magic. It is a mix of technical fixes, useful content, and patient link building. My journey from zero to first clients taught me that the basics are the foundation of everything.

If you are struggling with your organic visibility, do not overcomplicate it. Start with a technical audit, fix critical errors, create content that answers real questions, and earn links that build trust.
