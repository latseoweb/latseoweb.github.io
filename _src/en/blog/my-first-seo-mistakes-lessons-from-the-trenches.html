My first big SEO mistake was a URL change after which Google Ads sent visitors to a 404 page for four days. It taught me to check everything connected to the old address before changing anything. In this article I share this and other lessons from my first two years working in SEO.

How a URL change cost me my ad budget

I restructured a website's URLs but did not notice that Google Ads was still pointing to the old addresses. After the change, all the ads sent people to a 404 page. People clicked, but saw only an error message.

It took four days before I understood what had happened. All that time the clicks were being paid for, and I lost that ad budget.

The main lesson: before you change a URL, check what points to the old address. It could be ads, internal links, email campaigns or links on other websites. The old address must be redirected to the new one with a 301 redirect, so that neither people nor Google end up on a 404 page.

I check everything before any change

Now I run a check before and after every major change. This applies not only to URLs but also to content, meta tags and internal links. A simple technical check can be done with Screaming Frog, which finds links to pages that do not exist.

It is also important to understand why the changes are needed at all. When I was working on my own website, I realised the content was too informational and nobody was searching for it. I reworked the content and the SEO, and only then did traffic appear. The first organic visitors came after a month and a half.

SEO takes patience, but with the right foundation, results can appear sooner. Lazergriezeji.lv, which I built from scratch, started receiving organic visitors and calls from people interested in its products in less than a month.

Keywords are the foundation, but not everything

The second lesson is about keyword research. I use Google Keyword Planner to understand what people search for and where it is possible to compete. But search volume alone says nothing. It is more important to understand what your customer really wants to find.

My first client was a company I had previously worked for. I built websites for them, and by doing that practical work I also learned SEO. There I realised that SEO is not just the technical side. It is also content that answers people's questions.

Keywords are only the beginning. Just as important are the page structure, internal links and how easily the user finds what they need. I wrote about how to bring all of this together in one plan on the content strategy page.

E-E-A-T: the principle I ignored at first

At first I thought good content and keywords were enough. Only later did I appreciate the importance of E-E-A-T. The abbreviation stands for Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness and Trustworthiness.

Google uses these principles in its guidelines to assess whether content is created by people who know what they are talking about. That is why I now consider it one of the most important things I work on in clients' sites.

In practice, this means clear information about the author and the company, real experience in the text and accurate facts. That is why, in this blog, I write about my own work and mistakes rather than general theory.

My morning routine with Google Search Console

Every morning I open Google Search Console. I am first interested in the overall results for the whole website, not the ranking of a single keyword. Search Console shows how the whole domain is developing: impressions, clicks and pages that are rising or falling.

I check individual keywords when they matter to the client and we have set specific goals. This daily check helps me spot changes quickly and react before a problem grows.

Adapting to AI Overview

I change my SEO approach when search changes. The last time this happened was when Google introduced AI Overview — an artificial intelligence-generated summary above the regular search results.

So that clients' pages can appear there, I started optimising them according to GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) principles as well. This means the page has clear, short answers to specific questions that artificial intelligence can understand and use. The introduction of this article is written exactly that way.

SEO helps you get quality leads

For me, the most important benefit of SEO is not rankings in themselves. It is quality enquiries from people who are already looking for your service.

When you can see which pages bring the most enquiries, you can make better-informed decisions about budget and content. You can spend more time on the pages that work and review the pages that bring no results.

What I do now so the mistakes are not repeated

  • Before changing a URL, I check whether ads or other links point to the old address, and I set up a redirect.
  • Before and after changes, I check URLs, meta tags, internal links and content.
  • Every morning I look at Google Search Console.
  • In content, I show real experience and facts to strengthen E-E-A-T.
  • I build pages so that they are understandable to both Google and AI Overview.

SEO is not a task you can set up and forget. It has to be monitored, analysed and improved.
